# Deviations from protocol.md (all made 2026-11-22 BEFORE any model was trained or evaluated)
1. Download: California (06) and Colorado (08) failed to download (API timeouts on very large states); excluded. 49 of 51 requests succeeded; 47 states have wells after filtering.
2. Features: sampling depth is missing for 100% of wells in this API output, so it is dropped. Features are latitude, longitude and USGS aquifer code (42% present; "unknown" otherwise).
3. Evaluation units: the protocol's KMeans blocks are not fixed across partitions, which breaks paired tests. PRIMARY evaluation is now leave-one-state-out (state = fixed, independent unit; a state agency runs a campaign in its own state; cold-start setting) with a 25 km buffer; states need >=100 wells and >=15 unsafe wells. SECONDARY: a single KMeans partition (60 blocks, seed 0) as within-region robustness. Everything else (methods, metrics, budgets, tests) is unchanged.
4. Tuning: 8 states drawn at random (seed 0) are used ONLY for tuning (LightGBM random search, 20 trials; kernel bandwidth and shrinkage grids) and never as evaluation units. Their wells may be used as training data for other states.
5. Random-order baseline M0 is the analytic expectation (budget fraction x number of unsafe wells), not simulated.
6. Seeds: 5 LightGBM seeds (0-4), averaged per state before testing.
7. (added after the main runs) Nitrate (second contaminant) could not be downloaded: the API timed out for 47 of 49 states. It is NOT in the paper. Generality evidence is therefore limited to arsenic (>10 and >5 ug/L), two split designs, and a thinned-data check.

26. (2026-11-23, round 4) NEW METHOD ABF (adaptive bayesian-mixture feedback), specified before any run: 31 experts = {map-free: h in {2,5,10,20,50} km x kappa in {0.5,2}} + {map-based: same h x kappa in {0.5,2,10,50}} + {map-only}; expert prediction p = (kappa*prior + a_h)/(kappa + a_h + b_h) with prior = training prevalence (map-free) or the LightGBM map (map-based); weights = exp(-cumulative prequential log-loss) on the wells tested so far (learning rate 1, uniform start); wells ranked by the weighted mean prediction; first batch top of the map (or random if no map). No hyperparameter is tuned on any data. Variants: ABF without map (map-free experts only), ABF with fixed h = 5 km. Evaluated on every outcome with the same states, seeds and statistics as the main runs; all results reported.

33. (2026-11-23, round 4) ERROR FOUND AND CORRECTED. In the early runs the USGS aquifer code (a nominal label) was passed to LightGBM as an integer, so its arbitrary numeric order was treated as meaningful. When geology predictors were added, the code was changed to treat aq_code as categorical for ALL later runs, which silently made the earlier and later base runs inconsistent (e.g. static-map enrichment for arsenic >10 was 1.25 in the early run and 1.20 in the ABF run). Treating a nominal variable as categorical is the correct choice, so every run that used the numeric treatment was repeated with the categorical treatment: arsenic >10, >5, uranium 30 and 10, spatial blocks, thinned data, the added experiments (kappa/heuristics/warm start), the M7 check, the robustness checks, ABF, ABF tuning, ABF warm start, coverage and forward-in-time checks. Runs that already used the categorical treatment (geology/environment runs, fluoride, extended CA/CO) were not repeated. The superseded outputs are kept in results/superseded_numeric_aq/ and are NOT used anywhere in the paper. No method, threshold or state was changed; the tuning states are the same; hyperparameters are re-tuned because the feature treatment changed.

39. (2026-11-23, RECHECK stage 4 round 1, pre-registered BEFORE running) Idea 1, geographically weighted map training. For each held-out state the map is trained with sample weights exp(-(d/L)^2), d = distance (km) from a training well to the held-out state's centroid, L in {250, 500, 1000, 2000} km (all four reported); unweighted training is the reference. WHAT WOULD PROVE IT WRONG: gain < 0.20, or not significant after Holm, or not reproduced on fresh seeds or on arsenic > 5. Code: code/src/ge_weighted.py.
40. (2026-11-23, RECHECK stage 4 round 1, pre-registered BEFORE running) Idea 2, calibrated remaining-yield forecast. During an ABF campaign, after 10%, 20% and 30% of wells are tested, forecast the number of unsafe wells in the whole state as (found so far) + sum of ABF mixture probabilities over untested wells, with an 80% interval (normal approximation of a Poisson-binomial: mean +/- 1.2816 sd, sd^2 = sum s(1-s)). Baselines: naive scale-up (found / fraction tested) and map-only (found + sum of map probabilities over untested wells). Scenarios: arsenic > 10, arsenic > 5, fluoride > 2 (same states and seeds as ABF runs). Metrics: median relative error |forecast - truth| / truth per state; pooled coverage of the 80% interval. Hypotheses: (a) ABF forecast error is lower than both baselines at 20% tested in every scenario (paired Wilcoxon, Holm); (b) pooled coverage of the 80% interval lies between 70% and 90%. WHAT WOULD PROVE IT WRONG: error not lower than a baseline in a scenario, or coverage outside 70-90%. Code: code/src/abf_forecast.py.

(2026-11-23, RECHECK stage 4 round 2, pre-registered BEFORE running; follows the falsified coverage hypothesis of item 40, whose raw result was: pooled 80% interval coverage 23-27% for arsenic > 10) Conformal recalibration of the remaining-yield forecast. For each scenario and checkpoint (10, 20, 30% tested), a multiplier c is set to the 80% quantile of |truth - forecast| / sd over the TUNING states (all seeds) and the interval is forecast +/- c*sd on the evaluation states. The tuning states are never evaluation states. Hypotheses: (a) pooled coverage of the recalibrated 80% interval on evaluation states lies between 70% and 90% in arsenic > 10, arsenic > 5 and fluoride > 2; (b) the median full interval width divided by the true count at 20% tested is at most 1.0 (an interval wider than the truth is not useful). WHAT WOULD PROVE IT WRONG: coverage outside 70-90% in any scenario, or median relative width > 1.0. Code: code/src/abf_forecast.py (calibration part). 43. (2026-11-23, round 6, pre-registered BEFORE building the campaign table or running any method) REAL-CAMPAIGN REPLAY. Purpose: replace the straw-man random-order baseline with the order in which real USGS sampling campaigns actually tested their wells. Definitions: a campaign = all wells whose earliest valid result belongs to the same (state, Org_Identifier, Project_Identifier string); real order = chronological by the earliest-sample date, ties broken at random (mean over 20 tie-break draws); a campaign qualifies if it has >= 100 wells, >= 10 unsafe wells and >= 30 distinct sample dates (so that its order is informative). If fewer than 15 campaigns qualify for a contaminant/threshold, a pre-declared fallback is used instead: >= 60 wells, >= 8 unsafe, >= 20 distinct dates; whichever rule is used is reported. Methods applied to the same set of wells: real order, random order (analytic), static map M1 (trained on other states with the 25 km buffer, scenario tuning parameters), kernel feedback M4, follow-unsafe H1, ABF with the map. Scenarios: arsenic > 10 (primary), arsenic > 5, fluoride > 2, uranium > 10 (base predictors; same tuning files as the main runs). Unit of analysis: the campaign; sensitivity analysis: state means. Metric: enrichment at 20% tested (1 = random order), and tests per unsafe well. Hypotheses: (a) ABF and kernel feedback find more unsafe wells at 20% tested than the real chronological order in a majority of campaigns (paired Wilcoxon, Holm over method comparisons within a scenario); (b) real order is not itself far above random (median enrichment <= 1.3), i.e. historical sampling was not already close to an optimal order. WHAT WOULD PROVE THEM WRONG: ABF or feedback not significantly above real order after Holm, or real order already above 1.3 and not significantly below feedback. Code: code/src/campaign_replay.py; outputs results/replay/.
44. (2026-11-24, pre-registered BEFORE any code for this method was written or run) HIERARCHICAL ABF (ABF-H): the starting weights of ABF's 31 experts are learned from other states' campaigns instead of being uniform. Definition: for each donor state and seed, run the standard ABF (uniform start) and record each expert's cumulative log loss over the first 30% of that state's wells, divided by the number of wells tested (per-well loss l_k); delta_k = l_k - min_k l_k; delta_bar_k = mean over donor states and seeds. Prior weight pi_k proportional to exp(-tau * N0 * delta_bar_k), N0 = 300 wells, tau in {0 (= standard ABF), 0.1, 0.3, 1.0}. For a target state the donors are ALL OTHER states (tuning and evaluation states, never the target itself). tau is chosen on the TUNING states only (highest mean enrichment at 20% tested; ties go to the smaller tau) and then applied to the evaluation states. Everything else (experts, learning rate 1, batch 10, first batch top-by-map, map trained on other states with the 25 km buffer, units, seeds) is as in ABF. Scenarios: arsenic > 10, arsenic > 5, uranium > 30, uranium > 10, fluoride > 2, fluoride > 4 (5 seeds; 3 for fluoride); additionally batches of 50 and 200 wells for arsenic > 10 and fluoride > 2 (3 seeds). Comparators from the same code path: standard ABF; M1 map, M4 feedback, M5 hybrid and H1 follow-unsafe are taken from results/abf_<run>/abf.csv (same units and seeds). Statistics: paired Wilcoxon over states, Holm over the six scenarios. Hypotheses: (a) ABF-H has higher mean enrichment than ABF in every scenario and the gain is significant after Holm in at least arsenic > 10, where ABF's gap to the best fixed rule was largest (0.35); (b) the worst-case gap of ABF-H to the best fixed rule over the six outcomes is smaller than ABF's; (c) with batches of 200 wells ABF-H loses less than ABF. WHAT WOULD PROVE THEM WRONG: no significant gain in any scenario; worst-case gap not smaller than ABF's; ABF-H significantly worse than ABF in any scenario (this would be reported as a negative result and the idea dropped). Code: code/src/abfh.py, code/src/abfh_experiments.py; outputs results/abfh_<run>/.

48. (2026-11-24, pre-registered BEFORE running) TRAVEL-DISTANCE CHECK ON THE REPLAYED CAMPAIGNS.
