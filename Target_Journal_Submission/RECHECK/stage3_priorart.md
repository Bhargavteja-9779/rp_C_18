## Close calls (stated plainly)
1. ActiveRemediation (Abernethy et al., KDD 2018): same problem structure (choose which units to inspect under a high cost, using predictive models plus field results) for lead pipes. The principle that predictions plus feedback help is NOT new. Different: groundwater contaminants, held-out-state evaluation of transferred maps, contaminant dependence, ABF.
2. Adaptive cluster sampling (Thompson 1990): the classical form of my follow-unsafe and kernel-feedback rules. My contribution is not the rule but its measured size against maps on national well data.
3. Cao et al. 2024 and Usman et al. 2026: fluoride models transfer poorly without hydrochemical predictors; the arsenic result corroborates them.
4. MacDonald and Tippett 2020: neighbour-exceedance targeting exists in practice.
5. Yin et al. 2021: risk-based adaptive arsenic sampling design (static).
Search for new papers of the last 6-12 months: found OWL-GPS (2026), Azad et al. 2026, Usman et al. 2026, Su 2026, a 2026 New York lead-line audit; none does the held-out-state campaign comparison for private-well contaminants (within the limits of the sources above).
Other fields with the same structure: lead-pipe excavation, radon outreach, adaptive cluster sampling in ecology, technology-assisted review stopping rules (QPET/QBCB; snippet only).
## Positioning decision (close-call rule b)
The paper is repositioned honestly around what is different: a measurement on held-out states, the contaminant dependence, ABF with a standard loss bound, and the conditions under which adaptive testing fails. The Letter and cover letter now state that feedback is not a new idea.
