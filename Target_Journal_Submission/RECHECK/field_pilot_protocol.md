# Field pilot protocol (DRAFT, NOT RUN; for the authors and an agency partner)
Purpose: the largest remaining scientific gap is that all evidence is retrospective. This protocol tests whether ordering well tests adaptively finds unsafe wells faster than the agency's usual order, in a real campaign.
Design: cluster-randomised, paired by county or health district. Each participating programme supplies a candidate list of private wells it intends to test (address coordinates, no owner data in the analysis file). Programmes are randomised 1:1 to (A) usual order or (B) adaptive order (follow-unsafe rule, optionally ABF seeded with the programme's earlier results, using code/campaign_planner.py), with results returned to the planner in batches of 10 wells or fewer and laboratory turnaround recorded. Stratify by contaminant of concern (arsenic, uranium or fluoride).
Primary endpoint: unsafe wells found per 100 tests when 20% of the candidate list has been tested (the unit used in the paper). Secondary: tests per unsafe well, distinct 5 km areas with an unsafe well, time from sampling to result, refusals and unreachable wells, cost per unsafe well found. Pre-specify the analysis: paired or clustered Wilcoxon test across programmes; report confidence intervals; no subgroup claims.
Sample size (from the replay of real campaigns, results/replay/pilot_power.json; arsenic above 10 µg/L; paired difference in enrichment, two-sided alpha 0.05): follow-unsafe rule: mean difference 0.60, SD 1.00, about 22 programmes for 80% power (29 for 90%); kernel feedback: 33 (43); ABF: 52 (70). The replay campaigns had a median of 301 wells and 32 unsafe wells; programmes with far fewer candidate wells need more programmes. These are planning figures from historical surveys, not guarantees.
Ethics and data: well owners must consent to testing as in the normal programme; the planner needs only coordinates and results; check institutional review rules for the use of results (UNVERIFIED here). Safety: owners of wells with unsafe results receive results and guidance as usual; the adaptive arm must not delay any result.
Risks: laboratory turnaround longer than a few weeks reduces the gain (paper: Table of batch sizes); refusals may be non-random; contamination clustering may be weaker than in the USGS data (synthetic fields show no gain without clustering).
