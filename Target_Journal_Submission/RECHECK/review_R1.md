# R1 - the desk-reject editor (ES&T Letters), written before and after fixes
## The rejection email I would send
Dear authors, thank you for submitting 'Which Private Wells to Test First?'. We decline to send it for review. The central finding, that choosing the next well from the results of earlier wells finds more contaminated wells than a static map, is expected and follows from adaptive cluster sampling and from the lead-pipe literature; the weak transfer of groundwater models to new regions has been reported for fluoride. The paper is a simulation on archived USGS study wells, which are not a random sample of private wells, with no field trial and no new measurement. The proposed ABF rule is a standard Bayesian mixture whose bound concerns prediction loss, not discovery, and it was not the best rule in most outcomes. The scope of ES&T Letters is experimental environmental science and technology; this is a data-science evaluation. We encourage submission to a journal of environmental data science.
## Reasons and what was done (FATAL / MAJOR / MODERATE / MINOR)
- FATAL (cannot be fixed by analysis): no field trial or new measurement. Mitigation only: honest limits; the planner tool; recommendation of an agency pilot. NOT FIXED.
- MAJOR: novelty of the feedback idea. Fixed by repositioning: Letter and cover letter now state that feedback is not new, cite adaptive cluster sampling, active search, ActiveRemediation and the fluoride-transfer papers, and claim only the measurement, the contaminant dependence and ABF.
- MAJOR: scope. Measured: 14 of 157 ES&T Letters papers 2023-2026 mention ML/data-driven methods; 1 concerns private wells. Plausible, not typical. NOT FIXABLE; stated in the final report.
- MODERATE: ABF not best everywhere; the Letter says so ('a safe default, not a uniformly better rule').
- MODERATE: pooled AUC could mislead; fixed (within-state AUC 0.51 stated).
- MINOR: title length 14 words vs journal median 12.5.
Verdict after fixes: desk-rejection risk remains HIGH.
