# Pre-submission inquiry (DRAFT for the authors; whether ES&T Letters accepts inquiries is UNVERIFIED)
Subject: Pre-submission inquiry: ordering tests for private wells (simulation and replay of real campaigns)

Dear Editor,
Before submitting, we would like to ask whether a Letter of the following kind fits the scope of ES&T Letters.
Problem: about 40 million people in the United States use private wells that are tested at the owner's or an agency's initiative, and agencies can test only part of them.
What we did: using 53,294 arsenic, 28,117 uranium and 150,299 fluoride wells from USGS, we simulated testing campaigns with one state held out at a time and replayed 53 real historical sampling campaigns for arsenic.
Main findings: for arsenic and uranium a risk map from other states is close to random order in a new state; for fluoride it transfers; the order actually used in the historical campaigns found no more unsafe wells than random order, while adaptive reordering found about 1.4 to 1.6 times as many (tests per unsafe arsenic well 9.2 to 5.2 in simulation). We propose a rule that learns during the campaign how far to trust the map, with a standard loss bound.
Limits: archived study wells, no field trial, close prior work in adaptive sampling and lead-pipe programmes (cited).
Is this within scope for a Letter, or would you recommend a different journal? We can send the manuscript.
Sincerely, [corresponding author]
